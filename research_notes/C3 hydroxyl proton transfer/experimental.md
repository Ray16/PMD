# Experimental evidence on the C3 hydroxyl proton acceptor

## What do the experiments actually establish about Asp versus ATP oxygen?

### Takeaway

The experiments establish important catalytic and orientational functions for the conserved Asp, and demonstrate that phosphorylation can proceed without its carboxylate in at least one archaeal MDD. They do not directly identify the atom that receives the hydroxyl proton in wild-type MDD, bacterial PMD, or the RFD4 designs. The most discriminating evidence separates phosphorylated-intermediate formation from final decarboxylation product formation. [Motoyama et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/), [Chen et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7414129/)

### Inferences

- Confidence is high that Asp's importance to the complete reaction should not be equated automatically with direct general-base catalysis in the first step. This follows from the distinction between overall-product assays and intermediate-resolving experiments. [2004 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2279928/), [2017 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/)
- The evidence supports keeping a phosphate-assisted route as a serious hypothesis; it is insufficient to declare it the experimentally established or universally dominant path. [2017 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/), [2020 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7414129/)

### Gaps

- I found no directly observed C3-OH proton trajectory, acceptor-specific proton spectroscopic assignment, or experimental branching ratio for the two proposed paths in these primary studies.
- Targeted searches did not locate a proton-transfer kinetic isotope experiment resolving the acceptor in At/Ha PMD. This is a search limitation, not a claim that no such study exists.

## Do conflicting results or species differences prevent a universal mechanism assignment?

### Takeaway

The older Asp-base assignment and newer non-obligatory-Asp model differ in both experimental resolution and enzyme context. The clearest kinase-conversion result is from an archaeal MDD using MVAPP, not the bacterial PMDs and MVAP substrate in the user's project. [Barta 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4227304/), [Motoyama 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/)

### Cited Findings

- Chen and colleagues point out that phosphorylated-intermediate production by D281T/V is much slower than WT IPP formation, and discuss an orientational contribution of Asp. Motoyama and colleagues suggest that release of an abnormal intermediate may help explain small mutant turnover values. Consequently these steady-state rates are not clean measurements of intrinsic proton-transfer barriers. [Chen 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7414129/), [Motoyama 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/)
- Barta's structures show that deleting Asp reorganizes substrate binding. A mutant may therefore change both geometry and available reaction pathways. [Barta 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4227304/)

### Inferences

- Mutant rescue of phosphorylation logically permits, but does not prove, a WT Asp-assisted path with a different mutant bypass. This possibility is a limitation of mechanism inference from mutagenesis, not a reported demonstration of two pathways.
- Conversely, loss of final product after Asp substitution cannot by itself support a requirement for Asp-mediated initial deprotonation. A subsequent decarboxylation defect is an experimentally demonstrated alternative explanation in the archaeal system.
- PMD-specific evidence must be assessed independently before transferring an MDD mechanistic preference. Substrate charge, Mg coordination, active-site hydration, pH and protein sequence can affect the relative barriers.

### Gaps

- The selected primary experiments do not compare both proton-transfer paths within the same WT At/Ha PMD under the same substrate, metal and protonation conditions.
- No definitive experimental agreement on the precise proton-transfer timing follows from these studies: proton movement could be coupled to phosphoryl transfer rather than occurring as an isolated initial alkoxide-forming step.

## How should the project use these findings and what would discriminate the routes?

### Takeaway

The defensible statement is that the phosphate-assisted route is supported mechanistically but the actual proton acceptor in the project enzymes remains unresolved. Geometry plots should compare candidate access and should not be labeled as likelihoods or mechanism assignments.

### Cited Findings

- The 2020 experimental structural paper expressly discusses an unresolved proton acceptor and possible protonation of substrate through a dissociative transfer state. [Chen 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7414129/)
- The 2017 intermediate-resolving experiment illustrates why first-step product detection is more informative than final IPP loss for this question. [Motoyama 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/)

### Inferences

- Measure substrate O3···Asp carboxylate O and substrate O3···each nonbridging gamma-phosphate O. Report exact atom names and Mg contacts. A shorter O···O distance measures access; it does not determine basicity, proton orientation, transition-state timing or an activation free energy.
- Do not reject a design solely because its Asp is farther from the hydroxyl if phosphate-assisted phosphorylation remains viable; equally, retaining a nearby Asp cannot validate the complete catalytic machinery or the later decarboxylation step.
- A useful project experiment would quantify first-step 3-phosphorylated intermediate, ADP and final IP separately in WT and carefully chosen Asp substitutions, with protein integrity and binding controls. This can test whether Asp is required for first-step chemistry in the actual PMD system, but an alternative acceptor would still need identification.
- Solvent isotope/pH experiments could test proton involvement but would not automatically identify a specific acceptor; pH shifts can reflect ATP, metal or other site ionizations. For computation, compare explicit competing paths rather than assuming one, with matched protonation, metal hydration, substrate state and conformational sampling. These are proposed discriminating approaches, not experiments already performed here.

### Gaps

- No numerical probability that one mechanism dominates is justified by the evidence reviewed.
- Search coverage is a focused primary-source audit, not a registered systematic review. Queries covered Asp mutations, kinase-conversion intermediates, proton-transfer/isotope searches and later mechanistic references; broad searches through current indexed literature did not uncover a later direct experimental assignment.
