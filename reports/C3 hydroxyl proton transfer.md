# Evidence leaves PMD proton acceptance unresolved

**The present evidence does not establish whether ATP’s nonbridging γ-phosphate oxygen or a nearby protein base accepts the C3-OH proton in At PMD, Ha PMD, or the RFD4 designs.** ATP-assisted transfer is a credible hypothesis worth testing, supported by a published MDD calculation and compatible with experiments in related enzymes. However, the principal calculation did not compute a matched Asp-assisted barrier, so it cannot establish that ATP assistance wins that comparison. Asp mutants that retain phosphorylation show that its carboxylate is not always indispensable, but do not identify the proton acceptor in wild type. Direct PMD observations also leave room for a protein-assisted route. The appropriate project decision is therefore to retain both mechanisms, compare their geometries explicitly, and require enzyme-specific reaction-path or kinetic evidence before assigning the dominant route. ([McClory et al., 2020](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/ob/c9ob02254f), [Motoyama et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/), [Vinokur et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5177888/))

## Experiments separate Asp’s importance from its chemical task

Three questions must remain distinct: whether Asp is important for overall catalysis, whether its carboxylate is necessary for initial phosphorylation, and whether it receives the hydroxyl proton in the normal wild-type reaction. An experiment can answer the first without answering the other two. In particular, losing final product can result from a defect in substrate orientation, phosphorylation, phosphate elimination, decarboxylation, or another part of turnover. Conversely, a mutant that phosphorylates its substrate can use a route different from wild type.

The evidence matrix below separates observations from assignments. MVAPP denotes mevalonate 5-diphosphate, the substrate in classical MDD studies; MVAP denotes mevalonate 5-monophosphate, the substrate of the project’s PMDs. M3K and MBD are related enzymes that isolate phosphorylation and decarboxylation, respectively; their observations are informative comparisons rather than direct tests of the same reaction in At or Ha.

| Primary study and system | Observation relevant to the first step | What it establishes—and what remains open |
|---|---|---|
| [Krepkiy and Miziorko, 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2279928/): yeast MDD, MVAPP | Asp302 substitutions strongly reduced overall turnover while retaining nucleotide binding. | Asp is catalytically important. Final-product loss does not assign the proton acceptor. |
| [Barta et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4227304/): *S. epidermidis* MDD | Asp deletion altered the orientation of substrate carboxylate and C3-OH groups. | Supports a positioning role and is consistent with the proposed base role; does not directly observe proton transfer. |
| [Vinokur et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4315659/): *T. acidophilum* M3K, mevalonate | The related kinase performs C3 phosphorylation without the corresponding MDD Asp. | Demonstrates related chemistry can proceed without that Asp; does not identify PMD’s acceptor. |
| [Vinokur et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5177888/): *Roseiflexus* PMD, MVAP | At pH 4.3, conversion of MVAP stopped while decarboxylation of a supplied prephosphorylated intermediate remained active. | Constrains a phosphorylation-associated ionization; does not identify which group ionizes. |
| [Motoyama et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/): *S. solfataricus* MDD, MVAPP | D281T/V produced the 3-phosphorylated intermediate instead of final IPP. | Shows phosphorylation can occur without the Asp carboxylate in that enzyme; does not establish the WT proton acceptor. |
| [Thomas et al., 2019](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acschembio.9b00322): At and Ha PMD | Established substrate specificity and binding determinants; At ligand-bound structures did not resolve a bound nucleotide. | Directly relevant to the project enzymes, but does not determine the initial proton-transfer route. |
| [McClory et al., 2020](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/ob/c9ob02254f): modeled *S. epidermidis* MDD/MVAPP | Obtained a viable phosphate-assisted QM/MM phosphorylation path. | Supports feasibility in the chosen model; no matched Asp-path barrier was calculated. |
| [Chen et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7414129/): *E. faecalis* MDD, MVAPP | Active-crystal structures established closed states and two metal sites; the proposed mechanism includes phosphate proton acceptance. | Independent structural support for the catalytic arrangement, but the accepting atom remains unresolved. |

The older Asp-base interpretation was reasonable given its conservation, proximity, and mutational importance. Yet Barta’s crystallographic complexes lacked ordered Mg and placed the phosphoryl donor and substrate hydroxyl about 5.5 Å apart; the productive geometry was modeled rather than directly captured. Asp deletion also reorganized substrate binding. Thus the structural phenotype does not uniquely distinguish a missing general base from loss of a productive substrate pose. ([Barta et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4227304/))

The strongest experimental challenge to an obligatory Asp-base model is the phosphorylated intermediate from D281T/V. It isolates first-step chemistry from downstream failure. Nevertheless, mutant steady-state rates cannot be read directly as intrinsic phosphorylation barriers: formation, release, and altered binding can contribute. The study’s ¹³C-labeled mass-spectrometric experiment identifies the intermediate; it is **not a proton-transfer kinetic isotope effect**. A mutant bypass, water assistance, or another accepting group remains logically possible. ([Motoyama et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313113/))

## The calculation demonstrates feasibility without ranking both routes

McClory and colleagues reconstructed an MDD–MVAPP–ATP complex with two Mg ions and obtained a phosphate-assisted path whose estimated activation barrier was 17.4 kcal/mol. They optimized a transition-state structure and reported one relevant imaginary frequency. Crucially, after finding Asp283 distant in their chosen model, they did not pursue the catalytic-base mechanism. **There is no matched Asp-assisted barrier in that study from which to calculate a preference.** Its result supports a viable modeled route, not a head-to-head free-energy victory across competing paths or enzyme conformations. ([McClory et al., 2020](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/ob/c9ob02254f))

The independent 2020 structural study strengthens the case that metal organization, active-site closure, and Lys positioning matter to phosphoryl transfer. It also proposes that Asp helps orient the substrate and contributes to later chemistry. Importantly, its discussion expressly leaves the proton acceptor uncertain and suggests transferred phosphate as a candidate. A reaction drawing in that paper is a mechanistic model, not direct visualization of the moving proton. The authors also discuss the possibility that the substrate remains protonated before and during a dissociative transfer state. This cautions against assuming that a separate, completed deprotonation necessarily precedes the phosphoryl-transfer event. ([Chen et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7414129/))

For PMD, the low-pH result deserves attention rather than being fitted into a phosphate-only account. Selective suppression of phosphorylation is compatible with altered ionization of a nearby residue, as its authors proposed as one explanation. It is also compatible with changes involving phosphate protonation, metal coordination, or coupled site ionizations. Consequently it neither proves the protein-base route nor permits that alternative to be dismissed. ([Vinokur et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5177888/))

Transfer to At and Ha remains an additional uncertainty. Their direct characterization concerns MVAP recognition and enzyme activity, with meaningful differences in substrate specificity. Histidine substitutions mainly affected substrate affinity, supporting a binding role rather than identifying His as a general base. Nothing in those observations assigns the initial C3-OH acceptor. The extra phosphate and different active-site environment in an MDD/MVAPP model prevent treating its mechanism as experimentally established for PMD/MVAP. ([Thomas et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31268677/))

The local QM/MM plan is an explicit choice to test a substrate-assisted hypothesis. It is not an independent observation supporting that hypothesis. Its diagnostic calculations have documented protonation, solvent, metal-shell, and scan-validation issues, so they should not be presented as confirmation of either route. These limitations are recorded in the [project plan](../QMMM%20modeling/qmmm/docs/PLAN.md) and [structural review](../QMMM%20modeling/qmmm/analysis/structural_audit/REVIEW.txt).

## Both distances measure access rather than mechanism likelihood

The structural analysis should keep three different observables separate. The hydroxyl oxygen–Pγ distance and hydroxyl oxygen–Pγ–β/γ-bridge angle describe phosphoryl-transfer alignment. The hydroxyl oxygen–nonbridging γ-oxygen distances describe possible access for phosphate-assisted proton transfer. The hydroxyl oxygen–Asp carboxylate oxygen distances describe possible access to a protein-side-chain acceptor. Substituting Pγ for a γ oxygen would measure a different chemical question.

For these RFD4 files, the substrate hydroxyl is **IMV O16**, the electrophilic phosphorus is **IAT P41**, and the leaving-group bridge is **IAT O27**. The three nonbridging γ oxygens are **IAT O13, O16, and O17**. The repeated name O16 belongs to two different ligands, so selection must include the residue identity. Both Asp OD1 and OD2 should be retained, along with every γ-oxygen value; a nearest-oxygen summary must identify which atom supplied it. The [two-distance plot](../RFD4-proteina-designs/analysis/proton_acceptor_distances.png) and [active-site views](../RFD4-proteina-designs/analysis/active_sites/index.html) are companion structural outputs.

**A shorter O···O contact does not establish the lower reaction barrier.** The supplied structures lack the hydroxyl hydrogen, a resolved protonation assignment, and a complete mechanistically validated solvent environment. They therefore do not establish donor–H–acceptor orientation, the availability of an accepting lone pair, proton affinity, or the energetic cost of reorganizing the site. Mg coordination further changes the meaning of a phosphate contact. A nearby Asp can orient the hydroxyl even if it never receives the proton; a nearby phosphate oxygen can be geometrically accessible without being the kinetically preferred acceptor. These are reasons to interpret the distances as candidate access, not probabilities.

Asp is not the only chemically possible protein acceptor. A suitably positioned, appropriately protonated Glu or His could participate, and water can relay protons. Lys and Arg are usually protonated in this setting and should not be counted automatically as basic acceptors merely because a nitrogen is close. The coordinate screen therefore reports Asp/Glu carboxylate oxygens and His ring nitrogens separately, with protonation caveats.

A distant direct acceptor also does not exclude a water-mediated path. Conversely, adding an arbitrary hydrogen to make one favorable contact would build the desired mechanism into the starting model. Any protonated comparison needs consistent chemistry and validation before interpreting its geometry.

For the designs, a nearby Asp and two Mg ions are promising pocket features, but they do not establish the full catalytic machinery. Asp’s downstream contribution remains relevant even if ATP assists the first proton-transfer event: a distinct ATP-independent bisphosphomevalonate decarboxylase also requires its corresponding Asp for activity. Thus an ATP-assisted first step is not a rationale for removing Asp from a complete decarboxylase design. ([Aoki et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9254496/))

## The project can discriminate chemistry with matched tests

The most informative experimental next step is to separate initial phosphorylation from subsequent elimination/decarboxylation in the actual At/Ha systems. A proposed time-resolved assay would follow ADP, the 3-phosphorylated MVAP intermediate, and final IP separately in WT and selected Asp variants. Fold, binding, and background ATPase controls would be essential. Detecting first-step chemistry after Asp substitution would test its necessity in that PMD, while avoiding the ambiguity of an assay that reports only final product. It still would not, by itself, locate the proton on ATP.

Proposed pH profiles, solvent isotope effects, and proton inventories could then test whether proton transfer contributes to the measured kinetic step. Their interpretation would need an explicit kinetic model: a solvent isotope effect is not automatically an acceptor assignment, and an apparent pKa can reflect more than one ionization. Direct intermediate and proton-state observations, where technically achievable, would carry more weight than proximity or conservation alone. These are discriminating experiments to consider, not results already available for these designs.

The corresponding computational test should evaluate both paths in the **same enzyme–substrate system**, with matched reactant protonation, ATP/MVAP charge states, Mg stoichiometry and coordination, solvent, reactive region, and embedding. Multiple starting conformations matter because a single distant-Asp pose can exclude one route by construction. Sampling should follow both phosphoryl transfer and proton transfer, including a water relay if the site supports one. A proton-transfer coordinate based on donor–H and acceptor–H distances would reveal chemistry that an O–P scan alone cannot establish.

Before comparing barriers, each model must have chemically valid reactants and products. A stationary transition-state claim needs the relevant imaginary mode and connecting endpoints; a free-energy claim needs converged sampling, replication, and uncertainty. Metal-shell exchange or solvent relaxation must not be mistaken for the chemical event. The existing local validation requirements are a useful starting point for these checks, but do not supply a result for either competing mechanism. ([Project structural review](../QMMM%20modeling/qmmm/analysis/structural_audit/REVIEW.txt))

Asp is the closest plausible protein-side-chain acceptor in all eight structures. The nearest gamma oxygen is closer than Asp in seven groups; in G1, Asp is closer by only 0.080 Å. The largest distance difference is 0.389 Å in G6. This comparison does not establish a barrier ranking. The full measurement file preserves both Asp oxygens and all three gamma oxygens, rather than only these minima.

All eight structures also place a Lys terminal nitrogen 3.11–3.67 Å from a gamma-phosphate oxygen and contain two Mg ions. The nearby Asp and Lys atoms carry source coordinate-conditioning annotations (H6 and H4), so their presence should be interpreted in that generation context. This is a partial catalytic arrangement, not a validated complete catalytic network. Other contacts vary: the closest Arg/Lys contact to the MVAP carboxylate is 3.08 Å in G2 and 3.27 Å in G4, but ranges from 4.11 to 8.11 Å in the other groups. That contact was screened without assuming sequence-homologous residue identity. Missing explicit water and protonation information limits metal-shell and proton-relay assessment. Favorable attack geometry alone cannot establish that the later elimination/decarboxylation machinery is intact.

## Conclusion

For this project, ATP-assisted transfer should be treated as a serious, literature-motivated hypothesis, while protein/Asp assistance remains a live alternative. **There is insufficient evidence to name a dominant proton acceptor in At/Ha PMD or the RFD4 designs, and no justified numerical likelihood.** The strongest use of the new figures is to identify structures that can accommodate one or both candidate arrangements and expose missing interactions for testing.

The colleague-facing claim should therefore stay at the level the evidence supports: “We are comparing phosphate-assisted and protein-assisted proton-transfer geometries in the RFD4 pockets. A published MDD QM/MM path supports phosphate assistance, but it did not compare an optimized Asp path, and direct PMD-specific proton-acceptor evidence is lacking. Favorable distances identify hypotheses for matched mechanistic validation; they do not yet establish mechanism or catalytic superiority.” This report is a focused primary-source assessment, not an exhaustive systematic review.
